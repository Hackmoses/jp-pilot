In the Hall-Héroult process, why is cryolite (Na₃AlF₆) added to the alumina?

A
To act as the anode Wrong. The anodes are carbon blocks, which burn away in the oxygen.
B
To lower the melting point of alumina Your answer. Correct.
C
To remove iron oxide impurities Wrong. Iron oxide is removed earlier, in the Bayer process.
D
To reduce Al³⁺ ions to aluminium Wrong. Electrons at the cathode reduce Al³⁺, not cryolite.
WHY IT IS CORRECT

Pure alumina (Al₂O₃) melts at about 2050 °C, far too hot to run cheaply. Dissolving it in molten cryolite lets electrolysis run at about 960 °C, which saves a huge amount of energy.

THE TRAP

"To purify alumina" sounds right, but purifying happens before electrolysis, in the Bayer process. Cryolite is a solvent, not a cleaner.

REMEMBER IT

Cryolite Cuts the heat. 2050 down to 960.
