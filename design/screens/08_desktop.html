What mass of iron is produced when 80 g of Fe₂O₃ is completely reduced by CO in the blast furnace? (Fe = 56, O = 16)

A
28 gForgets that one Fe₂O₃ gives two Fe.
B
56 gYour answer. Correct.
C
112 gDoubles the moles of Fe twice.
D
40 gHalves the mass instead of using moles.
WATCH IT SOLVED Step 3 of 4
M(Fe₂O₃) = 2(56) + 3(16) = 160 n = 80 ÷ 160 = 0.5 mol
1 Fe₂O₃ → 2 Fe, so 0.5 mol → 1 mol Fe
mass = 1 × 56 = 56 g

"Each Fe₂O₃ holds two iron atoms, so half a mole gives one whole mole of iron."

THE WORKING
  1. Molar mass of Fe₂O₃ = 160 g/mol
  2. Moles = 80 ÷ 160 = 0.5 mol
  3. Ratio 1 : 2 gives 1 mol Fe
  4. Mass = 1 × 56 = 56 g
THE TRAP

28 g is the most common wrong answer. It comes from forgetting that one Fe₂O₃ gives two Fe atoms.

Where does the 2 in "2 Fe" come from?
Look at the formula Fe₂O₃. The small 2 after Fe means each unit holds two iron atoms. When all the oxygen is removed, both irons are left, so one Fe₂O₃ gives 2 Fe.
