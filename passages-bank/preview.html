Watching our thoughts · summary · JAMB 2011

PDF pp. 26–27 · key p. 45

PASSAGE II

COMPREHENSION: Read each passage carefully and answer the questions that follow it

  1. Like a clock with the pendulum in full swing, the mind moves as fast as time.
  2. But we ought to mind our thoughts for if they turn to be our enemies, they will be too many for us and will drag us down to ruin.
  3. But some people may say that they cannot help having bad thought even though they sting like vipers.
  4. That may be so, but the question is, do they hate them or not?
  5. We cannot keep thieves from looking in at our window, but if we open our doors to them and receive them joyfully, we are as bad as they.
  6. We cannot help the birds flying over our head; but we may keep them from building their nets in our hair.
  7. Vain thought will knock at the door but we must not open to them.
  8. Though bad and evil thought rise in our hearts, they must not be allowed to reign.
  9. He who turns a morsel over and over in his mouth dose so because he likes the flavour, and he who meditate upon evil, love it, and is ripe to commit it.
  10. Think of the devil, and he will appear, turn your thought toward evil and your hands will soon follow.
  11. Snails leaves their slime behind them, and so do vain thought.
  12. An arrow may fly through the air, and leaves no trace, but an evil thought always leaves a trail like a serpent.
  13. Where there is much traffic of bad thinking, there will be much mire and dirt.
  14. Every wave of wicked thought adds something to the corruption which rots upon the shore of life.
  15. It is dreadful to think that a vile imagination, once indulge, gets the key of our minds, and can get in again very easily, whether or not we let it in, and what may follow, no one knows.
  16. Nurse evil on the laps of thought, and it will grow into a giant.
  17. Therefore; there is wisdom in watching every day, the thought and imagination of our heart.
  18. Good thoughts are blessed guest and should be welcome, and much sought after, but bad thought must fly out as swiftly as they moved in.

1. JAMB 2011 Q8

From the argument in the second paragraph, it can be concluded that evil thought control the lives of people who

Answer D · inference

  • (a) Are helpless because they fly out of their minds The writer says people can refuse to dwell on thoughts.
  • (b) cherries idle and slothful ways The point is about what people choose to keep thinking about, not about laziness.
  • (c) are thieves with evil instincts The thieves are a comparison for thoughts that look for a way in.
  • (d) treasures and ruminate on them Correct. The writer compares dwelling on an evil thought to keeping food in the mouth because you like it.

2. JAMB 2011 Q9

The expression ‘Think of the devil and he will appear…..., as used in the passage suggests that

Answer C · word meaning in context

  • (a) likes the devil evil thoughts must not reign in our hearts This gives general advice. The expression shows what can happen when the advice is ignored.
  • (b) evil thoughts are fantasies which exist only in people’s mind The writer connects thought with action, so it does not stay only in the mind.
  • (c) uncontrolled evil thoughts may lead to evil deeds Correct. The next words say your hands will follow. A repeated thought can turn into a deed.
  • (d) the devil gives evil thoughts only to those who invite him in The writer uses the devil as an image. The later words explain the link between thinking and acting.

3. JAMB 2011 Q10

which of the following statement summarizes the argument of the fourth paragraph

Answer B · summary

  • (a) heavy traffic on a miry and dirty road may lead to evil thoughts Traffic and dirt are images for the effects of bad thinking. Road traffic is not the cause.
  • (b) the more evil we think, the more vile we are likely to become Correct. This gathers the repeated images into one idea. Repeated bad thoughts strengthen harmful habits.
  • (c) evil people should not be welcome as guest in our homes the same way we welcome good people The passage is about welcoming or rejecting thoughts, not about choosing house guests.
  • (d) evil thoughts control the key to human heart and no one can keep them out The writer says bad thoughts may return easily. The passage still urges readers to keep them out.
The medical definition of miscarriage · cloze · JAMB 2012

PASSAGE IV

The passage below has gaps numbered 16 to 25. Immediately following each gap, four options are provided. Choose the most appropriate options for each gap. Each question carries 3 marks

  1. The medical definition of miscarriage is the spontaneous loss of a pregnancy before twenty-four weeks.
  2. Miscarriage is very common, occurring in ten to twenty per cent, of conformed pregnancies.
  3. Most of these feature [16] twelve week of pregnancy.
  4. The most common [17] is vaginal bleeding, which can range from light spotting to heavier than a period.
  5. You may see blood clots, brown discharge or other tissues that are not [18] identifiable.
  6. Sometimes a sac-like structure is seen.
  7. Often, there is cramping with pelvic or back pain.
  8. You may find that the usual symptoms of pregnancy, such as breast tenderness, feeling sick and having to pass urine more [19] than usual stop unexpectedly.
  9. Sometimes there are no signs or symptoms of miscarriage and pregnancy symptoms continue, and the miscarriage is only [20] in a routine scan.
  10. About half of all early miscarriages happen because of a problem in the way the genetic material from the egg and sperm have combined during [21].
  11. It can be difficult to find out why this has [22], but it is more likely to be due to random chance than to any underlying problem with either parent.
  12. Imbalances in pregnancy hormones, problems in the immune [23], and some serious infections are also thought to make miscarriages more likely.
  13. The risk of miscarriage [24] with age because the quality of eggs deteriorates.
  14. If a woman drinks too much alcohol or smokes heavily, the risk of miscarriage is higher.
  15. It is also increased with [25] pregnancies such as twins.

Adapted from Saturday Punch, 13 October, 2007

1. JAMB 2012 Q16

Most of these feature ...16... twelve week of pregnancy.

Answer B · cloze vocabulary

  • A. In the penultimate Penultimate means next to last. The paragraph is describing the early weeks.
  • B. In the first Correct. The phrase identifies the first twelve weeks rather than one particular week.
  • C. around Around suggests an approximate point. The phrase refers to the whole early period.
  • D. For For would tell how long something lasts. The gap asks when it happens.

Gap clue: feature … twelve week of pregnancy

2. JAMB 2012 Q17

The most common ...17... is vaginal bleeding, which can range from light spotting to heavier than a period.

Answer D · cloze vocabulary

  • A. type Bleeding is an indication in this sentence, not a category of miscarriage.
  • B. Cause The text names a sign a person might notice, not what causes the event.
  • C. Period Period refers to a time span or to menstruation. The sentence singles out a sign.
  • D. Symptom Correct. Bleeding is the sign the sentence describes. Symptom is the word for that sign.

Gap clue: The most common … is vaginal bleeding

3. JAMB 2012 Q18

You may see blood clots, brown discharge or other tissues that are not ...18... identifiable.

Answer A · cloze vocabulary

  • A. clearly Correct. Clearly identifiable means easy to recognise. That fits a description of what a person may see.
  • B. naturally Naturally does not say whether the tissue can be recognised easily.
  • C. directly Directly implies a particular method of identifying something. No method is given.
  • D. Medically The sentence describes what a person may see. It gives no clinical test or diagnosis.

Gap clue: other tissues that are not … identifiable

4. JAMB 2012 Q19

You may find that the usual symptoms of pregnancy, such as breast tenderness, feeling sick and having to pass urine more ...19... than usual stop unexpectedly.

Answer B · cloze grammar

  • A. painfully The phrase contrasts how often something happens, rather than how painful it is.
  • B. frequently Correct. More frequently than usual compares how often someone needs to pass urine.
  • C. gradually Gradually tells how slowly a change happens. It does not give the needed frequency.
  • D. Commonly Commonly describes how widespread something is. Frequently fits how often an individual does it.

Gap clue: having to pass urine more … than usual

5. JAMB 2012 Q20

Sometimes there are no signs or symptoms of miscarriage and pregnancy symptoms continue, and the miscarriage is only ...20... in a routine scan.

Answer C · cloze vocabulary

  • A. prevented A scan can reveal what has happened. It does not prevent the event in this sentence.
  • B. managed Managed would imply treatment. The clue is that there were no obvious signs.
  • C. discovered Correct. The scan is how the event becomes known when there are no visible signs.
  • D. Stopped The sentence describes detection. It does not say the scan stops the event.

Gap clue: no signs or symptoms … only … in a routine scan

6. JAMB 2012 Q21

About half of all early miscarriages happen because of a problem in the way the genetic material from the egg and sperm have combined during ...21....

Answer D · cloze vocabulary

  • A. pregnancy Pregnancy names the broader period, not the event in which the egg and sperm combine.
  • B. incubation Incubation does not name the combination of the egg and sperm.
  • C. mating Mating does not name the joining described in the sentence.
  • D. Fertilization Correct. Fertilization names the event in which the egg and sperm combine.

Gap clue: genetic material from the egg and sperm have combined during …

7. JAMB 2012 Q22

It can be difficult to find out why this has ...22..., but it is more likely to be due to random chance than to any underlying problem with either parent.

Answer A · cloze vocabulary

  • A. occurred Correct. Occurred means happened. The sentence asks why the event happened.
  • B. enlarged The sentence is not about something becoming larger.
  • C. continued The sentence asks why the event happened, not why it kept happening.
  • D. emerged Emerged often means came into view or became known. The sentence asks for the cause of the event.

Gap clue: find out why this has … but it is more likely to be due to random chance

8. JAMB 2012 Q23

Imbalances in pregnancy hormones, problems in the immune ...23..., and some serious infections are also thought to make miscarriages more likely.

Answer D · cloze vocabulary

  • A. syndrome Syndrome is a group of symptoms. It does not name the body's defence system.
  • B. process Immune process is too vague for the part of the body meant here.
  • C. response An immune response is a reaction. The sentence refers to a wider system in which problems may occur.
  • D. system Correct. Immune system is the usual name for the body's defences.

Gap clue: problems in the immune …

9. JAMB 2012 Q24

The risk of miscarriage ...24... with age because the quality of eggs deteriorates.

Answer A · cloze vocabulary

  • A. increases Correct. Deteriorates means becomes worse. The sentence links that worsening with a higher risk.
  • B. starts The risk exists already. The sentence describes a change with age.
  • C. reduces Reduces means becomes lower, which goes against the reason given.
  • D. appears Appears would mean the risk begins to show. The sentence is about its level changing.

Gap clue: with age because the quality of eggs deteriorates

10. JAMB 2012 Q25

It is also increased with ...25... pregnancies such as twins.

Answer C · cloze vocabulary

  • A. complicated Twins are given as an example of more than one baby, rather than of every complication.
  • B. advance Advance does not describe the number of babies in the example.
  • C. multiple Correct. Twins are an example of a pregnancy with more than one baby.
  • D. confirmed Confirmed tells whether a pregnancy is established. It does not explain the example of twins.

Gap clue: pregnancies such as twins

Pottery Training Centre · comprehension · JAMB 2013

PDF pp. 69–70 · key p. 87

PASSAGE I

COMPREHENSION: Read each passage carefully and answer the questions that follow it

  1. In 1951, the Government decided to start a Pottery Training Centre where new and more advanced technical methods, especially glazing, could be taught.
  2. The centre was intended to serve the whole of the defunct Northern Region, and there were several reasons for choosing Abuja.
  3. The first was the excellence of the traditional pottery made in the Emirate.
  4. Secondly, fire-wood is plentiful; this is a most important consideration, because in the making of glazed pottery, more firewood than clay is required.
  5. Thirdly, there are good clays, and good local sources for the raw materials needed for the glazes.
  6. Fourthly, water, which is an-other important raw material, is plentiful.
  7. Finally, Abuja is in a central position for the whole region and is a town where learners from many different parts can find a congenial temporary home, and where the Emir and his Council are actively interested in the project.
  8. Nearly all the making is done by a process called ‘throwing’, so called because the lumps of clay are thrown by the potter onto a wheel-head.
  9. They are weighed out so that each pot will be roughly the same size; for example, for making pint-sized jugs, the lumps of clay will be one and a half kilogrammes.
  10. The potter sits on the saddle of the wheel and spins it by pushing a pedal with his left foot.
  11. He has a bowl of water, a loofah, a bamboo knife, a pointed stick or porcupine quill, a wooden smoothing tool which potters call a rib, and a piece of wire-like object that is used for wedging.
  12. He makes the wheel-head slightly damp, and throws the lumps into the middle.
  13. The first work is to force the lump to the centre, then he presses his thumb into the middle of the lump, using water to keep it slippery.
  14. When the bottom is of the right thickness, he begins to draw up the walls until they are of the right height.
  15. Then he shapes the belly and shoulder of the pot.
  16. He trims off any waste clay.
  17. In this way, a small and medium-sized pot can be made more quickly and accurately.

1. JAMB 2013 Q2

Which of the following is true according to the passage?

Answer B · literal detail

  • A. Anyone, with almost no training, can run pots on a wheel. The passage describes a skill and a training centre. It makes no claim about working without training.
  • B. Pots can be made quickly and correctly. Correct. Sentence 17 says pots can be made more quickly and accurately.
  • C. A pot thrown on a wheel is less likely to break. The passage says nothing about pots breaking less often.
  • D. The potter does not have to work hard if he uses the wheel. Speed and accuracy tell you the result. They do not tell you how hard the work is.

2. JAMB 2013 Q3

From the passage, how does a potter make several pots of almost identical size?

Answer B · literal detail

  • A. By having the knowledge of different pots. The question asks for a specific step. Knowledge of different pots is too general.
  • B. By weighing the lumps of clay. Correct. The potter weighs out the clay so that the pots will be roughly the same size.
  • C. By having the right tools The tools help shape and smooth the clay. The passage gives another step for size.
  • D. By knowing what to do from experience. Experience may help, but the passage names weighing as the way to keep size consistent.

3. JAMB 2013 Q4

The phrase trims off any waste clay, as used in the passage, means to---

Answer A · word meaning in context

  • A. cut away unnecessary parts Correct. After shaping the pot, the potter removes extra clay.
  • B. force the clay to the centre The potter centres the lump earlier in the process.
  • C. divide the clay into two Trimming removes what is extra. It does not split the pot.
  • D. wash away different colours. The passage describes shaping clay. It says nothing about washing away colours.

4. JAMB 2013 Q5

The word congenial, as used in the passage, means---

Answer D · word meaning in context

  • A. congested A crowded home would hardly be a reason to choose the town.
  • B. precise Precise means exact. Here the word describes a place to stay.
  • C. similar The sentence does not compare the home with another home.
  • D. nice Correct. The sentence gives a reason Abuja suited learners. A nice or pleasant place to stay fits.
The moralist and the economist · summary · JAMB 2016

PDF pp. 126–127 · key p. 144

PASSAGE I

COMPREHENSION: Read the passage carefully and answer the question that follows. The question carries 3 marks

  1. The African moralist has always regarded smoking as an indication of moral degradation.
  2. A number of people have accepted the moralist idea on smoking while a good many people have remained indifferent to the moralist view and have continued to smoke.
  3. The same argument has been applied to the consumption of alcohol.
  4. The African moralist, basing his judgement on the behaviour of a few alcoholics, tend to regard the habit of taking alcohol as a sign of wretchedness.
  5. The moralist holds the view that anybody who forms the habit of consuming alcohol will never do well in life.
  6. While this may be true in respect of a few people in the society, the fear of the moralist has not been justified.
  7. However, the economist is primarily interested in the habit of smoking and the consumption of alcohol in so far as they give satisfaction to smokers and drinkers and so generate supply of and demand for tobacco and alcohol.
  8. Some moral principles associated with religion tend to lead on to economic problems.
  9. Followers of certain religions are expected not to consume pork, take alcohol or smoke tobacco.
  10. Devotees of some religious groups, on the other hand, can eat pork while others are expected to abstain from alcohol and smoking.
  11. Strict observance of these moral rules could cripple the breweries, the cigarette factories and some businesses; however, there seems to be a growing number of alcohol consumers and cigarette smokers-a development which should be of interest to the economist.

Adapted from Peter Scott's article in Sunday Times.

1. JAMB 2016 Q3

According to the passage, the moralist idea is that

Answer A · literal detail

  • (a) the smoking of cigarettes is bad and unacceptable Correct. The moralist calls smoking a sign of moral degradation.
  • (b) it is typically African not to smoke cigarettes The passage describes a viewpoint. It does not call abstinence typical of all Africans.
  • (c) people should accept a point of view only when they are convinced The paragraph says people respond differently. It gives no rule about when to accept a view.
  • (d) smoking is not good but a little alcohol may be permitted The moralist also condemns the drinking habit and offers no exception for a small amount.

2. JAMB 2016 Q4

Which of the following statements is true according to the passage?

Answer B · literal detail

  • (a) total abstinence from drinking and smoking is a religious obligation The passage limits these rules to followers of certain religions.
  • (b) smoking and drinking may have positive effects on the economy Correct. From the economist's perspective, demand supports supply and related businesses.
  • (c) every one ignores the moralist view on drinking and smoking The passage says some people accepted the moralist's view.
  • (d) people who drink or smoke surely die of cancer The passage gives no claim about cancer.

3. JAMB 2016 Q5

The positions maintained by the moralist and the economist can be described as being

Answer D · summary

  • (a) quite indifferent Both take specific positions, so neither is indifferent to the subject.
  • (b) very agreeable The moralist condemns the habits. The economist considers how they affect demand and supply.
  • (c) very passionate The passage contrasts what the two people judge. It does not compare the strength of their feelings.
  • (d) at variance Correct. The moralist judges the habits morally. The economist considers their effect on supply and demand.
The geography master · comprehension · JAMB 2018

PDF pp. 157–158 · key p. 167

PASSAGE A

Read the following passage and answer the questions that follow.

  1. Returning home after a decade and half abroad, our Geography master remained incurably addicted to foreign ways and ideas for years after landing here.
  2. He would forever stick to his theory of Africans suffering from a curse inflicted on them by the Almighty God for some heinous sins committed centuries ago.
  3. He would, in support of this theory, ask listeners: "Why would our mosquito inflict deadly malaria on us whereas the British mosquito does not bite?
  4. Why aren't there poisonous snakes in Britain whereas here most snakes are deadly?
  5. Why should the deadly sickle cell disease be peculiar to the black race?"
  6. Of course, he hardly waits for answers to these and similar questions before jumping to the same inevitable conclusion.
  7. However, he met his match one day when a new student joined the class and heard the litany we were used to.
  8. The new boy calmly said, "Sir, I happen to know a few white men who suffer from the sickle cell disease; some are Italians and some are Spanish.
  9. The mosquito is equally deadly in India, South-east Asian Countries and South America.
  10. The United States and some other South American Countries have their deadly snakes.
  11. And, Sir, I know many white men, some of them British, who would prefer our brilliant sunshine to their horribly cold winter."
  12. And he sat down.
  13. I had never, before that day, seen our master so consumed with anger.
  14. He directed a burning look at the poor boy, who had no answer to this new battle.
  15. Without as much as saying a single word, the master stalked out of the classroom.
  16. Needless to say, our anger was turned on the new boy, who had decided to rock the boat without taking the time to sound the water.
  17. A delegation was sent to the master to apologise to him.
  18. He was appeased.
  19. But we all noticed something rather unusual thereafter never again did he dwell on the issue of Africans being the cursed People.

1. JAMB 2018 Q1

What point of view is the Geography master fond of advancing?

Answer C · main idea

  • A. Africans are infested with all kinds of problem This is too general. It leaves out his explanation for the problems.
  • B. Only the white men are free from deadly diseases The master gives examples to back a wider claim about a curse.
  • C. The Almighty God is punishing Africans for sins they committed long ago. Correct. Sentence 2 gives the master's theory directly.
  • D. God did not curse the white people. This leaves out what the master says happened to Africans and why.

2. JAMB 2018 Q2

Which of the following arguments did he not use to support his vies?

Answer B · literal detail

  • A. In Africa, the mosquito causes deadly malaria whereas in Britain, it doesn't bite or caw e malaria Sentence 3 shows he contrasts the African and British mosquitoes.
  • B. There is malaria both in Britain and in African Countries Correct. The master claims the British mosquito does not bite. He does not say both places have malaria.
  • C. The snakes in Africa are deadly but those in Britain are harmless Sentence 4 shows he relies on a contrast between snakes in the two places.
  • D. The sickle cell disease is peculiar to the black race. Sentence 5 shows he treats sickle cell disease as peculiar to one group.

3. JAMB 2018 Q3

"...rock the boat" What figure of speech is this expression?

Answer B · word meaning in context

  • A. Simile It does not make a comparison using words such as like or as.
  • B. Metaphor Correct. The boy upset the usual order in class. The boat stands for that situation.
  • C. Personification The expression does not give a thing human qualities.
  • D. Hyperbole The expression makes an indirect comparison. It is not an exaggeration.

4. JAMB 2018 Q4

What extra argument (lid the new boy offer after countering each of vie master's points?

Answer D · literal detail

  • A. There are problems especially in Africa He gives examples outside Africa. This is not his extra point.
  • B. There are deadly snakes be the in America and Africa Deadly snakes are part of his reply to the teacher's earlier claims.
  • C. There are harmless snakes in Britain He answers the snake claim with examples from the Americas.
  • D. Many white men prefer the African climate to their own. Correct. After the replies about disease and snakes, he describes people who prefer the sunshine.

5. JAMB 2018 Q5

Why do you think the master fought back with his look rather than with further argument?

Answer A · inference

  • A. He knew that the boy's points were valid Correct. The student gives counterexamples. The master leaves without replying and does not repeat the claim.
  • B. He had answers to the boy's argument The passage shows him looking angry and leaving without an answer.
  • C. He went out to sort for the boy's argument The passage gives no sign that he left to look for a reply.
  • D. He already made up his mind on his points. The master had a firm view earlier. That alone misses why he stops defending it after this exchange.
