math-jamb-0016 · JAMB 2011 Q4 · PDF page 11 · Chapter 2 · Simple interest

A man invested ₦5,000 for 9 months at 4%. What is the simple interest?

A. ₦150
Nine months is three quarters of a year, so the interest is three quarters of ₦200.
B. ₦220
A full year earns ₦200. A 9-month investment at the same rate earns less.
C. ₦130
Multiply ₦200 by 9/12 to get ₦150.
D. ₦250
₦250 would require 15 months at ₦200 per year.

At 4% per year, ₦5,000 earns 5000 × 4100 = ₦200 in a year. For 9 months the interest is ₦200 × 912 = ₦150.

⚠ THE TRAP
Use the same time unit as the annual rate. Entering 9 as the number of years overstates the interest.
Remember it
Simple interest = principal × rate × time in years.
math-jamb-0017 · JAMB 2011 Q7 · PDF page 11–12 · Chapter 3 · Equations

If log3 18 + log3 3 − log3 x = 3, find x.

A. 1
With x = 1 the log argument is 54. It must be 27.
B. 2
Dividing 54 by 2 gives 27, whose logarithm to base 3 is 3.
C. 0
The term log3 x requires x > 0.
D. 3
54 ÷ 3 = 18, whereas 33 = 27.

Use the log laws to write log3 (54x) = 3. Therefore 54x = 33 = 27, so x = 2. This satisfies x > 0.

⚠ THE TRAP
Adding logs multiplies their arguments. Subtracting log3 x divides by x.
Remember it
Log plus means multiply. Log minus means divide.
math-jamb-0018 · JAMB 2011 Q13 · PDF page 13 · Chapter 5 · Remainder theorem

Find the remainder when x3 − 2x2 + 3x − 3 is divided by x2 + 1.

A. 2x − 1
Subtracting the product of the divisor and x − 2 leaves 2x − 1.
B. x + 3
The divisor times x − 2 contributes x − 2 at degrees one and zero. Subtract those terms to get 2x − 1.
C. 2x + 1
The constant subtraction is −3 − (−2) = −1.
D. x − 3
Join the x terms as 3x − x = 2x and the constants as −3 + 2 = −1.

The quotient is x − 2. Multiplying it by x2 + 1 gives x3 − 2x2 + x − 2. Subtract this entire product from the dividend to get 2x − 1.

⚠ THE TRAP
Keep the subtraction sign on the whole product. The last step is −3 − (−2).
Remember it
A quadratic divisor leaves a remainder of degree below two.
math-jamb-0019 · JAMB 2011 Q17 · PDF page 13 · Chapter 6 · Inverse variation

T varies inversely as the cube of R. When R = 3, T = 281. Find T when R = 2.

A. 118
Multiplying 281 by (32)2 gives 118. The question requires a cube.
B. 112
The constant TR3 is 23, so division by 8 gives 112.
C. 124
For R = 2, divide 23 by 23 = 8 to get 112.
D. 16
Dividing 23 by 4 gives this result. The denominator should be 8.

Write T = kR3. The given values give k = 281 × 27 = 23. At R = 2, T = 23 ÷ 8 = 112.

⚠ THE TRAP
The exponent is 3. Using an inverse square would produce 118.

math-jamb-0020 · JAMB 2011 Q19 · PDF page 13–14 · Chapter 7 · Linear inequalities

Solve the inequality −6(x + 3) ≤ 4(x − 2).

A. x ≤ 2
For example, x = −2 satisfies x ≤ 2 but fails the original inequality.
B. x ≥ −1
Expanding and rearranging gives −10 ≤ 10x.
C. x ≥ −2
At x = −2 the left side is −6 and the right side is −16, so the inequality fails.
D. x ≤ −1
At x = 0 the original inequality is true, and this choice excludes 0.

Expand to get −6x − 18 ≤ 4x − 8. Add 6x and 8 to both sides to obtain −10 ≤ 10x. Divide by positive 10 to get x ≥ −1.

⚠ THE TRAP
The inequality reverses only when you multiply or divide by a negative number.
Remember it
Keep the x coefficient positive to avoid a sign flip.
math-jamb-0021 · JAMB 2011 Q23 · PDF page 14 · Chapter 9 · Closure

A binary operation ⊕ on real numbers is defined by x ⊕ y = xy + x + y. Find 3 ⊕ (−23).

A. −12
The positive remainder after adding −2 and 3 is 1. Subtracting 23 leaves 13.
B. 13
The product is −2 and the remaining terms are 3 and −23.
C. −1
The sum −2 + 3 is positive 1, and subtracting 23 still leaves a positive result.
D. 2
Use the signed product −2 and include both input terms.

With x = 3 and y = −23, the product xy is −2. Then xy + x + y = −2 + 3 − 23 = 13.

⚠ THE TRAP
The ⊕ symbol uses the rule in the question. Keep the minus sign on y in both places.
Remember it
For this rule, write three terms: product, first input, second input.
math-jamb-0022 · JAMB 2011 Q27 · PDF page 15 · Chapter 11 · Polygons

What is the size of each interior angle of a 12-sided regular polygon?

A. 120°
A regular 12-sided polygon turns through 30° at each corner, leaving an interior angle of 150°.
B. 150°
Subtract the 30° exterior angle from 180°.
C. 30°
The question asks for the interior angle, which is 180° − 30°.
D. 180°
A straight angle includes both the interior angle and the 30° exterior angle.

The 12 equal exterior angles sum to 360°, so each is 30°. Each interior angle is supplementary to its exterior angle, giving 180° − 30° = 150°.

⚠ THE TRAP
Dividing 360° by 12 gives the turn at a vertex, which is the exterior angle.
Remember it
Interior angle = 180° − exterior angle.
math-jamb-0023 · JAMB 2011 Q30 · PDF page 15 · Chapter 12 · Volumes

A solid metal cube of side 3 cm is placed in a rectangular tank of dimensions 3 cm, 4 cm and 5 cm. What volume of water can the tank now hold?

A. 48 cm3
The cube’s volume is 3 × 3 × 3 = 27 cm3.
B. 33 cm3
The cube occupies 27 cm3 of the 60 cm3 tank.
C. 60 cm3
The cube takes up part of the space, so subtract its volume.
D. 27 cm3
The water fills the space left over after subtracting the cube from 60 cm3.

The tank’s volume is 3 × 4 × 5 = 60 cm3. The cube fits inside and takes up 33 = 27 cm3. The remaining capacity is 60 − 27 = 33 cm3.

⚠ THE TRAP
The amount removed is 27 cm3. The question asks how much space remains.
Remember it
Water capacity = tank volume − solid volume.
math-jamb-0024 · JAMB 2011 Q31 · PDF page 15–16 · Chapter 13 · Loci

The perpendicular bisector of a line segment XY is the locus of a point

A. whose distance from X is always twice its distance from Y.
Every point on the perpendicular bisector has a 1:1 distance ratio to the endpoints.
B. whose distance from Y is always twice its distance from X.
Even the midpoint of XY has equal distances to X and Y.
C. which moves on the line XY.
The two lines meet at a single point. XY itself generally contains points closer to one endpoint.
D. which is equidistant from the points X and Y.
Equal distances to X and Y characterise the entire perpendicular bisector.

Let M be the midpoint of XY. A point P on the perpendicular bisector forms right triangles PMX and PMY with equal legs PM and MX = MY. Their hypotenuses are equal, so PX = PY. Conversely, PX = PY places P on the perpendicular bisector.

⚠ THE TRAP
The line XY joins the endpoints. Its perpendicular bisector crosses it at right angles at the midpoint.
Remember it
Perpendicular bisector means equal distances from the two endpoints.
math-jamb-0025 · JAMB 2011 Q32 · PDF page 16 · Chapter 14 · Straight line

The midpoint of P(x, y) and Q(8, 6) is (5, 8). Find x and y.

A. (2, 10)
Averaging 2 with 8 gives 5, and averaging 10 with 6 gives 8.
B. (2, 8)
Averaging 8 with 6 gives 7. To get 8 as the average, the missing y must be 10.
C. (2, 12)
The y coordinates must sum to 16. Here 12 + 6 = 18.
D. (2, 6)
Two y coordinates of 6 have midpoint y = 6. The target is 8.

The midpoint formula gives x + 82 = 5 and y + 62 = 8. Thus x = 10 − 8 = 2 and y = 16 − 6 = 10.

⚠ THE TRAP
The midpoint y coordinate belongs halfway between the endpoints. Copying 8 as the missing y coordinate gives the wrong average.
Remember it
Missing endpoint = twice the midpoint − known endpoint.
math-jamb-0026 · JAMB 2011 Q35 · PDF page 16 · Chapter 15 · Bearings

A man walks 100 m due west from X to Y, then 100 m due north to Z. Find the bearing of X from Z.

A. 195°
X is east of Z, so the bearing is between 090° and 180°.
B. 135°
The direction from Z to X is southeast with equal eastward and southward distances.
C. 225°
To return toward X from Z you move right on a north-up map, not left.
D. 045°
X is south of Z. An angle of 045° points north of east.

From Z, X is 100 m east and 100 m south. Equal components make the direction 45° south of east. Bearings are measured clockwise from north, so add 45° to 90° to get 135°.

⚠ THE TRAP
The bearing of X from Z starts at Z. Reversing the start and end points rotates the bearing through 180°.
Remember it
From tells you where to stand. Then turn clockwise from north.
math-jamb-0027 · JAMB 2011 Q37 · PDF page 16 · Chapter 16 · Quotient rule

Find the derivative with respect to θ of sin θcos θ.

A. sec2 θ
The quotient rule gives sin2 θ + cos2 θcos2 θ = sec2 θ.
B. tan θ cosec θ
The quotient rule leaves a squared cosine in the denominator, giving sec2 θ.
C. cosec θ sec θ
The denominator in the quotient rule is cos2 θ.
D. cosec2 θ
The original quotient is tan θ, whose derivative is sec2 θ. The derivative of cot θ is −cosec2 θ.

The quotient rule gives cos θ · cos θ − sin θ · (−sin θ)cos2 θ. The numerator is cos2 θ + sin2 θ = 1. The derivative is therefore sec2 θ where cos θ ≠ 0.

⚠ THE TRAP
The derivative of cos θ is −sin θ. The quotient rule subtracts that term, so the numerator is a sum of squares.
Remember it
Quotient rule: denominator times numerator derivative minus numerator times denominator derivative, over denominator squared.
math-jamb-0028 · JAMB 2011 Q39 · PDF page 17 · Chapter 18 · Definite integration

Evaluate ∫01(3 − 2x) dx.

A. 3
The integral of −2x over the same interval is −1. Include it to get 2.
B. 5
The line’s height on [0, 1] never exceeds 3, so a unit-wide region cannot have area 5.
C. 2
The antiderivative 3x − x2 changes by 2 between the limits.
D. 6
The integrand drops from 3 to 1 over an interval of width 1, so its average height is 2.

An antiderivative is 3x − x2. Its value at 1 is 3 − 1 = 2. Its value at 0 is 0. Subtract to get 2.

⚠ THE TRAP
The integral includes the decreasing term −2x. Keeping only the constant term treats the graph as a rectangle of height 3.
Remember it
Definite integral = antiderivative at upper limit minus antiderivative at lower limit.
math-jamb-0029 · JAMB 2011 Q47 · PDF page 18 · Chapter 22 · Permutation

In how many ways can the letters of the word ELATION be arranged?

A. 6!
ELATION has seven distinct letters, so there are seven choices for the first position.
B. 7!
All seven letters differ, giving 7 × 6 × 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 arrangements.
C. 5!
There are seven positions to fill, including the vowels and consonants.
D. 8!
Count E, L, A, T, I, O and N. There are seven letters.

ELATION has seven distinct letters. The choices drop from 7 for the first slot to 6 for the next, and so on until one letter remains. Their product is 7!.

⚠ THE TRAP
Count all seven letters before using a factorial. No repeated-letter division is needed here.
Remember it
Arrange n distinct objects in n! orders.
math-jamb-0030 · JAMB 2011 Q49 · PDF page 19 · Chapter 23 · Probability

Find the probability that a number picked at random from the set {43, 44, 45, …, 60} is a prime number.

A. 23
Only 43, 47, 53 and 59 are prime.
B. 13
Test odd candidates for divisibility by 3, 5 and 7 before counting them.
C. 29
There are four prime numbers among the 18 possible choices.
D. 79
There are 14 composite choices, but the four primes are the favourable outcomes.

There are 60 − 43 + 1 = 18 equally likely integers. The primes are 43, 47, 53 and 59. So the probability is 418 = 29.

⚠ THE TRAP
Include both endpoints. Count prime outcomes in the numerator and all outcomes in the denominator.
Remember it
Probability = favourable outcomes ÷ total equally likely outcomes.
